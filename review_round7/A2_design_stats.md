## 1. Signal defensibility of the remifentanil HYPERAESTHESIA "signal" (a = 10 one-patient series)

【Problem】 The remifentanil HYPERAESTHESIA cell (a = 10) is driven 9/10 by a single 76-year-old man's repeated submissions, so framing it as a per-drug "signal" is not scientifically defensible; the manuscript states this honestly in the text but the Table 2 asterisk still presents it as a clean signal.

【Evidence】 `13_report_series_hyperaesthesia.csv`: of 10 remifentanil HYPERAESTHESIA reports, nine (safetyreportid 24402565, 24641950, 24675457, 24690033, 24715261, 24716978, 24726643, 24727039, 25115900) share country = US, age = 76, sex = M, and the same 5–7 perioperative products; the tenth (19700005) is a Japanese woman. Recomputed: 9/10 = 90 % of the cell is one patient. Table 2 (line 249) marks `HYPERAESTHESIA … 10 … 4.73* (2.54–8.80)` with `*` = "meets the signal criterion." The text is honest: §3.3 ("That comparison does not survive inspection … the ten reports therefore describe two patients"), §4.1 ("the corrected picture is no estimable signal"), §4.5 ("the corrected hyperalgesia comparison rests on ten reports describing two patients"). The contradiction is only visual/structural: a reader scanning Table 2 sees an unqualified `*`.

【Why it matters】 A table-level `*` is the unit editors and meta-readers extract. Stating in prose that it is not a signal while printing `*` invites the very misreading the rest of the paper works to prevent, and is exactly the artefact the manuscript's own Conclusion warns against ("a zero … is an artefact of terminology"). For the three comparator opioids (fentanyl a = 315, sufentanil a = 22, morphine a = 262; `01_faers_results.csv`) the HYPERAESTHESIA signal is genuine and large, so "met the signal criterion for all four opioids" (§3.3) is true by the count rule but masks that only 3/4 are real. Note also that the same case series contaminates the *comparators'* 2024 counts (§3.7, `20_2024cluster_membership.csv`: 7/7 sufentanil and 5/17 fentanyl 2024 HYPERAESTHESIA reports come from it), so the within-year comparator elevations are partly the same patient too.

【Specific fix】 In Table 2, change the HYPERAESTHESIA remifentanil row's footnote marker from a plain `*` to a distinct symbol (e.g. `†‡`) carrying the note: "Remifentanil a = 10 is nine reports for one 76-year-old man plus one Japanese woman (Table S9); not an estimable signal — see §3.3." Keep the `*`-definition footnote for the genuine comparator signals. Add one sentence to §3.3: "Only the fentanyl (a = 315), sufentanil (a = 22) and morphine (a = 262) HYPERAESTHESIA signals survive the case-series disclosure; the remifentanil one does not."

## 2. Sparse cells: interval failure and an unaudited signal gap

【Problem】 The stated signal criterion's `a ≥ 3` guard applies only to the ROR clause, not to the PRR (≥ 2 with χ² > 4) or IC (IC025 > 0) clauses; consequently two genuinely sparse cells — HYPERPATHIA fentanyl (a = 2) and HYPERPATHIA sufentanil (a = 1) — are marked as signals in Table 2 yet are omitted from the sparse-interval audit (`15_sparse_intervals.csv`, Appendix A1.4), which covers only four cells.

【Evidence】 Table 2 line 250: `HYPERPATHIA … 8.24* (1.99–34.06) [fentanyl, a = 2]` and `75.63* (10.41–549.63) [sufentanil, a = 1]`. The signal criterion (§2.4, line 69) is "(a ≥ 3 AND ROR-LB > 1) OR (PRR ≥ 2 AND χ² > 4) OR (IC025 > 0)." For HYPERPATHIA sufentanil a = 1: ROR-LB (Woolf) = 10.41 > 1 and PRR = 75.62 ≥ 2 with χ² > 4, so the PRR clause fires despite a = 1 < 3. Recomputed χ² for a = 1, b = 6512, c = 42, d = 20 686 132: χ² = N(ad−bc)²/((a+b)(c+d)(a+c)(b+d)) ≈ 72 > 4 → PRR clause satisfied. For a = 1 the IC bound is also not protective at N = 20.7 M: IC = log2(aN/((a+b)(a+c))) = log2(20.7 M/(6513·43)) = 6.21, Var(IC) ≈ (1 + 1/6513 + 1/43)/ln(2)² = 2.13, sd = 1.46, IC025 = 6.21 − 1.96·1.46 = 3.35 > 0 → IC clause also fires. So a = 1 passes both non-ROR clauses. `15_sparse_intervals.csv` contains only: HYPERAESTHESIA remi (a = 10), ALLODYNIA remi (a = 1), HYPERAESTHESIA 2024 (a = 8), PAIN remi (a = 23) — it does **not** contain either HYPERPATHIA row. Appendix A1.4 line 697 states "All four intervals exclude one for HYPERAESTHESIA, so that conclusion does not depend on the interval method," but that claim is scoped to the four audited cells only.

Stability check on the omitted cells (recomputed): for HYPERPATHIA sufentanil a = 1, the central-hypergeometric tail at null OR = 1 gives P(X ≥ 1) = 1 − P(X = 0); via the exact ratio recurrence P(X = 1)/P(X = 0) = (a+b)·c / (M − (a+b) − c + 1) = 6513·42 / 20 686 133 = 0.0132, so P(X = 0) ≈ 0.987 and P(X ≥ 1) ≈ 0.013 < 0.025. Because P(X ≥ 1) at OR = 1 is already below 0.025, the exact conditional lower bound lies **below 1**, so the cell cannot support a lower-bound-exceeds-1 signal under exact/mid-P methods. (I could not reproduce the exact numerical lower bound with `scipy.stats.nchypergeom_fisher` at N = 20.7 M — the non-central hypergeometric underflows at this population size; the analytic tail bound above is decisive and does not depend on that tool. INCOMPLETE: exact numeric bound not reproduced; analytic bound is.) For HYPERPATHIA fentanyl a = 2: E[X] = (a+b)·c/M = 121 819·41/20.7 M = 0.241, so P(X ≥ 2) ≈ 0.025 at OR = 1 — i.e. the exact lower bound sits near 1, borderline; it likewise needs explicit exact-method reporting and is omitted from the audit.

【Why it matters】 Disproportionality at a = 1 or a = 2 is dominated by sampling noise; the manuscript's own A1.4 demonstrates the principle (ALLODYNIA remi a = 1 → exact LB 0.088, "no direction read"). Printing `*` on HYPERPATHIA a = 1 / a = 2 while excluding them from the audit is an inconsistency that, if any reader treats `*`-rows as confirmed signals, overstates the hyperalgesia-related signal set. It also undercuts the "all four intervals exclude one" reassurance, which is true only for the four audited cells.

【Specific fix】 Apply the `a ≥ 3` minimum uniformly across all three clauses (or replace the count rule with "exact conditional or mid-P lower bound > 1"), and re-audit every signalled cell. Concretely: (i) add HYPERPATHIA fentanyl (a = 2) and HYPERPATHIA sufentanil (a = 1) to `15_sparse_intervals.csv` with exact/mid-P/Haldane bounds (HYPERPATHIA sufentanil will show exact LB < 1 and should lose its `*`, or carry a "sparse — not a signal" marker); (ii) in Appendix A1.4 replace "All four intervals exclude one for HYPERAESTHESIA" with "All four audited intervals exclude one for HYPERAESTHESIA; the two HYPERPATHIA signalled cells (a = 1, a = 2) are sparse and fail exact/mid-P methods (Appendix A1.4, table X)."

## 3. Head-to-head RORR covariance correction

【Problem】 None of substance. The covariance correction is implemented correctly (delta method over the eight disjoint drug×term cells), and it changes no headline conclusion. One interval's significance flips, which the manuscript reports but under-states as "no conclusion changes."

【Evidence】 `_r6_covariance.py` lines 51–94: it partitions the corpus into the eight cells {r1, r0, f1, f0, b1, b0, e1, e0}, computes gradients gR and gC of ln ROR for each drug, and sets Var(ln RORR) = Σ(gR−gC)²·cell (lines 78–87) — the correct delta-method variance for correlated ratios sharing the same corpus remainder. I re-derived the gradients and they match the code exactly (e.g. d ln ROR_R/d r1 = 1/a_R, d/d b1 = 1/a_R − 1/c_R; d ln ROR_C/d b1 = 1/a_C − 1/c_C). Internal consistency also checks: for PAIN vs fentanyl, `se_published` = 0.209316, `se_corrected` = 0.209055, and 2·`cov_log` = −1.095e-4 = `se_corrected`² − `se_published`² = 0.043704 − 0.043813 (recomputed). `18_rorr_covariance.csv` values match the manuscript's stated movements: HYPERAESTHESIA vs sufentanil 0.260–1.161 → 0.323–0.933; PROCEDURAL PAIN vs fentanyl 1.135–3.389 → 1.160–3.316; PAIN vs fentanyl 0.044–0.100 either way; DRUG INEFFECTIVE vs fentanyl 0.493–0.653 → 0.494–0.653. Recomputed CI for the corrected PAIN vs fentanyl from `se_corrected` = 0.209055: ln(0.0665) ± 1.96·0.209055 = (−3.122, −2.301) → (0.0441, 0.1001), matching the file.

【Why it matters】 This is the one place a reviewer could have found an error; it is correct. The only nuance: the corrected HYPERAESTHESIA vs sufentanil interval (0.323–0.933) **excludes 1** whereas the published (0.260–1.161) included it — i.e. the correction makes remifentanil significantly *lower* than sufentanil for HYPERAESTHESIA, a significance change the text acknowledges as "the largest movement" but wraps into "no conclusion changes." Since no claim rests on that ratio, it is immaterial, but "no conclusion changes" is literally false for that one interval's significance.

【Specific fix】 In Appendix A1.5 line 701, change "No conclusion changes" to: "No conclusion that the paper rests on changes; one interval's significance flips — the corrected HYPERAESTHESIA vs sufentanil bound (0.323–0.933) now excludes 1, whereas the independent-interval bound (0.260–1.161) included it — but this ratio is not used for any claim."

## 5. Negative-control validity of NAUSEA / VOMITING / PRURITUS / CONSTIPATION

【Problem】 None of substance on honesty, but the framing can be strengthened: these four terms are labelled "comparator terms … not negative controls in the causal sense" (§2.3), which is correct; however they are not even valid as *evidential* negative controls, because remifentanil's brief intra-operative exposure genuinely produces fewer nausea/pruritus/constipation events than chronic morphine — an exposure difference, not only a reporting-setting difference.

【Evidence】 §2.3 line 65: "They are not negative controls in the causal sense: a ratio below one shows that remifentanil is reported less, not that it causes less, and they are confounded by the reporting setting like any other term." §4.3 line 139: "remifentanil's brief intra-operative exposure may also yield fewer of these events, so the two cannot be fully separated." So the manuscript already acknowledges the exposure component. What it does *not* state explicitly is the stronger point: because remifentanil truly causes less nausea/pruritus/constipation (short exposure, no outpatient chronic use), their low reporting is partly a **real pharmacological difference**, so they cannot serve even as a demonstration that "remifentanil under-reports everything uniformly because of reporting setting." The DRUG INEFFECTIVE reversal (FAERS 0.568 vs fentanyl, but Canada 1.277/1.703 — Table 3) is the manuscript's own evidence that the under-reporting pattern is *not* a uniform artefact; the four comparator terms being low is likewise *not* purely a reporting artefact. The logic chain "remifentanil under-reports PAIN and 11/12 comparator-term ratios → reporting setting, not drug" (§3.4, §4.1) is therefore over-reaches slightly, because the comparator terms carry genuine exposure signal.

【Why it matters】 If a reader treats the four terms as negative controls (the name "comparator terms" and the "established, non-paradoxical opioid effects" description invite it), they may conclude the uniform under-reporting is purely a coding/setting artefact. It is not — part of it is real pharmacology. This does not damage the paper's core thesis (the PAIN result is term-dependent, shown by INADEQUATE ANALGESIA reversal, `21_alternative_proxy_terms.csv`), but the negative-control language should not imply causal nullness.

【Specific fix】 In §2.3, after "not negative controls in the causal sense," add: "Nor are they informative negative controls in the evidential sense: remifentanil's short intra-operative exposure genuinely yields fewer nausea, vomiting, pruritus and constipation events than chronic morphine, so their low ratios mix a real exposure difference with any reporting artefact; they are shown only to illustrate cross-term direction, not to establish a reporting-setting effect."

## 6. Confounding vs mediation: reporting depth and indication stratification

【Problem】 The manuscript's labelling of reporting depth as a mediator/collider (not a confounder) is correct. The Mantel–Haenszel depth adjustment is therefore a sensitivity bound, not a causal estimate — which the manuscript states, but the MH-adjusted point estimates (PAIN vs morphine 0.978, vs fentanyl 0.640) must not be read as the "true" deficit. On indication: holding indication constant moves the PAIN point estimate toward 1 only in the n = 4 pain stratum; in the n = 84 perioperative stratum (the actually-relevant one) it stays at 0.399 < 1, so indication does not robustly abolish the deficit.

【Evidence】 `cv/cv_depth_strata.csv`: PAIN crude RORR vs fentanyl 0.235 → MH 0.640 (0.027–14.898); vs morphine 0.146 → MH 0.978 (0.042–22.751). Table 6 footnote (line 380): "Reporting depth is a consequence of the reporting setting, not a pre-existing confounder, so it is conditioned on here as a sensitivity analysis for the opportunity to record — not as a control for confounding." Correct. But conditioning on a mediator/collider biases the causal estimate; the manuscript says "the adjustment bounds the mechanical component of the deficit rather than removing it" — acceptable, provided the MH point estimate is not presented as causal. `cv/cv_indication_strata.csv`: PAIN vs fentanyl 0.235 (all) → 0.399 (perioperative, n = 84) → 1.791 (pain, n = 4); vs morphine 0.146 → 0.161 (perioperative) → 1.416 (pain, n = 4). The pain stratum rests on remifentanil n = 4 with a = 1 PAIN event. Recomputed RORR for pain stratum vs fentanyl: a_re = 1, n_re = 4, a_fen = 99, n_fen = 631 → consistent with the printed 1.791 (0.184–17.397). The point estimate > 1 is driven by 1 event in 4 reports and the CI (0.184–17.397) is uninformative, as the manuscript states (§3.6). Crucially, the perioperative stratum (n = 84, the setting remifentanil actually occupies) gives 0.399 < 1 — so "holding indication constant" does **not** move it to unity; only the tiny pain stratum does. §4.3 line 139 ("holding indication and reporting depth constant attenuates but does not abolish the deficit") is the honest summary and is accurate.

【Why it matters】 Two risks: (i) a reader may read the MH-adjusted 0.978 (PAIN vs morphine) as "no deficit once depth is controlled" — it is a collider/mediator-conditioned bound with a CI of 0.042–22.751, i.e. compatible with a 23-fold deficit; (ii) the n = 4 pain stratum's point estimate of 1.791 could be cherry-picked as "no under-reporting in the pain indication" despite n = 4. Both are mitigated by the manuscript's caveats but worth tightening.

【Specific fix】 In Table 6, add a row note: "MH-adjusted estimates condition on a mediator of the setting→recording pathway and are sensitivity bounds, not causal estimates; their wide CIs preclude reading 0.978 (vs morphine) or 0.640 (vs fentanyl) as absence of a deficit." In Table 5, change the pain-stratum note from "an interval too wide to inform" to: "n = 4 remifentanil reports (one PAIN event); point estimate 1.791 is not interpretable and must not be read as absence of under-reporting."

## 8. Multiple comparisons

【Problem】 Minor. The manuscript states no multiplicity correction was applied and supplies a post-hoc Bonferroni, but the Bonferroni scope (72 drug–term comparisons) is narrower than the selective-testing issue created by adding five proxy terms after seeing zeros.

【Evidence】 Table 2 footnote (line 261): "No multiplicity correction was applied to this table; with a Bonferroni correction across all 72 drug–term comparisons the lower bound of the remifentanil HYPERAESTHESIA interval remains above one (1.61)." Recomputed Bonferroni lower bound for HYPERAESTHESIA remi (18 terms × 4 drugs = 72; α = 0.05/72 = 6.94e-4; z ≈ 3.42): ln(4.7299) − 3.42·0.31671 = 0.471 → exp = 1.60, matching the manuscript's 1.61. The Bonferroni is correctly computed. However, the deeper multiplicity issue is HARKing: the five proxy terms (HYPERAESTHESIA, HYPERPATHIA, PROCEDURAL PAIN, CHRONIC PAIN SYNDROME, DRUG WITHDRAWAL SYNDROME) were selected *after* the five planned strings returned zero (§2.3, Table S6 "Amendment 1, 16 Sep 2026"), so they are data-driven and the manuscript already says they "cannot be treated as independent confirmation" (line 560). The Bonferroni over 72 does not address that selective addition. Also note §3.4's "eleven of the twelve computable ratios were below 1, the exception being pruritus versus sufentanil" — recomputed: the four comparator terms × three comparators = 12 ratios, only PRURITUS vs sufentanil (1.310) > 1, so 11/12 is correct; this is a different scope from the 26 computable ratios in Table S5 and is internally consistent (the "twelve" = comparator terms only).

【Why it matters】 Bonferroni on 72 is a post-hoc reassurance that the remifentanil HYPERAESTHESIA signal survives, but that signal is the one the manuscript itself discredits as a case series (Issue 1). The real multiplicity exposure is the a-posteriori proxy addition, which the manuscript acknowledges but does not formally account for. Low impact on conclusions.

【Specific fix】 In the Table 2 footnote, append: "The five proxy terms were added after the planned strings returned zero (Table S6) and are data-driven; the Bonferroni above covers the 72 planned-and-proxy comparisons but does not compensate for the selective addition of proxies, which are reported descriptively rather than as confirmation."
